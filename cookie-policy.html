What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. When you return, the browser sends it back, which is how a site can remember something about your previous visit. Similar technologies include local storage, which works the same way from your point of view but is stored differently by the browser.

Cookies set by the website you are visiting are called first-party. Cookies set by another company whose content appears on the page, such as an advertising network, are called third-party.

Cookies that expire when you close your browser are session cookies. Cookies that stay until a set date are persistent cookies.

Your choices, and how to change them

The consent banner

On your first visit you will see a banner at the bottom of the page with three options:

  • Accept all. Analytics and advertising cookies may be set.
  • Reject optional. Only strictly necessary storage is used. Nothing on this site is withheld from you as a result.
  • Manage preferences. Accept or refuse analytics and advertising separately.

Until you choose, no optional cookie is set. Closing the banner without choosing is treated as a refusal.

Changing your mind

Select Cookie Preferences in the footer of any page. The banner reopens with the preference panel already showing, and your new choice replaces the old one immediately.

Opting out directly with advertising vendors

  • Google. Visit Google Ads Settings at adssettings.google.com to opt out of personalized advertising from Google.
  • Digital Advertising Alliance. Visit aboutads.info for a multi-vendor opt-out tool covering participating US companies.
  • Network Advertising Initiative. Visit optout.networkadvertising.org for a similar tool covering NAI members.

Those tools are operated by third parties, not by us, and how they work is up to them. Opting out of personalized advertising does not stop you seeing ads; it stops them being selected using your browsing history.

Browser and device controls

Every mainstream browser lets you block third-party cookies, block all cookies, or delete stored cookies, through its own settings. Blocking all cookies will break parts of many websites, including the part of this one that remembers your consent choice, which means we will have to ask you again on every visit.

Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track

We honor the Global Privacy Control signal. If your browser or an extension sends it, we treat it as a valid opt-out from the sharing of personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising, and advertising cookies are not set.

There is no agreed industry standard for the older Do Not Track browser signal, so like most sites we do not respond to it. Use the consent banner or GPC instead, both of which we do act on.

California residents

Selecting Reject optional is a valid exercise of your right to opt out of sharing under the CCPA as amended by the CPRA. See the California section of the privacy policy for your other rights, and the Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information link in the footer of every page.
