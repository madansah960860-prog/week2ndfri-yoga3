Sequence · 10 min

Ten minutes, before the day starts.

Twelve positions that move the spine in every direction it has and end you standing. Mat only. Written so it can be done in whatever you slept in.

Go to the timer

Photograph via Unsplash

About this practice

This is the sequence we recommend first, and the reason is entirely practical: it is short enough that it survives a bad week. A ten-minute practice done four or five mornings a week accumulates into something. A sixty-minute practice you keep rescheduling does not.

The order moves the spine through flexion, extension, rotation and side-lengthening in the first four minutes, then puts weight through the hands and feet, then finishes standing. It ends standing on purpose. A practice that ends lying down at seven in the morning is a practice you have to negotiate your way out of.

Ten minutes you actually do beats an hour you keep meaning to. 10-Minute Morning

Nothing in it requires a warm body, so it works first thing. The holds are deliberately short, between forty and sixty seconds, which is long enough to arrive in a shape and notice something and short enough that nothing starts to ache.

Read the beginner path further down before your first run through it. Every position in the order has a simpler option, and taking the simpler option is not a reduced version of this sequence. It is this sequence.

The essentials

Total duration. 10 min

Difficulty. Beginner

Positions. 12

Props required. A mat. Nothing else.

Space needed. About 7 feet by 3 feet.

Best time to use it. Before the day starts, in whatever you slept in.

The order, position by position

Total running time 10 min. Every line carries its hold time and a beginner path.

  1. 1 min Child's Pose Bālāsana Knees wide, big toes together, forehead resting on the floor or on a book. Let the breath move into the back of the ribs. Beginner path: Blanket between the heels and the hips, and a cushion under the forehead.
  2. 1 min Cat and Cow Marjaryāsana and Bitilāsana On hands and knees. Exhale and round the spine, inhale and let the chest lift. Roughly eight unhurried rounds. Beginner path: Sit in a chair with your hands on your knees and do exactly the same movement.
  3. 45 sec Thread the Needle, right side Parśva Bālāsana From hands and knees, slide the right arm under the body and rest the right shoulder and the side of the head on the floor. Beginner path: Keep the hips stacked over the knees and rotate half as far.
  4. 45 sec Thread the Needle, left side Parśva Bālāsana Same on the second side. Notice that the two sides are not the same and do not try to make them match. Beginner path: Blanket under the knees, and a cushion under the head.
  5. 1 min Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Hips up and back, knees generously bent, spine long. Heels do not need to reach the floor. Beginner path: Hands flat on a wall at hip height and walk the feet back instead.
  6. 1 min Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana Right foot forward between the hands, left knee down, hips settling forward. Hands on the floor or on blocks. Beginner path: Blanket under the back knee, hands on two books for height.
  7. 1 min Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana Second side. This is usually where a day spent sitting makes itself known at the front of the back hip. Beginner path: Blanket under the back knee, hands on two books for height.
  8. 45 sec Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Feet hip-width, knees soft, folding from the hip joints and letting the head hang. Hold opposite elbows if you like. Beginner path: Bend the knees a lot and rest your hands on your shins.
  9. 45 sec Mountain Pose Tāḍāsana Roll up to standing. Weight even across both feet, ribs settled over the pelvis, crown of the head tall. Beginner path: Stand with your back lightly against a wall.
  10. 40 sec Tree Pose, standing on the left leg Vṛkṣāsana Right foot to the inner left calf or thigh, never against the side of the knee. Fix your eyes on one still point. Beginner path: Right toes stay on the floor, left fingertips on a wall.
  11. 40 sec Tree Pose, standing on the right leg Vṛkṣāsana Second side. Falling out and stepping back in is the practice, not a failure of it. Beginner path: Left toes stay on the floor, right fingertips on a wall.
  12. 40 sec Mountain Pose to finish Tāḍāsana Back to standing still. Five or six even breaths, eyes open, and then get on with the day. Beginner path: Sit tall on the front edge of a chair with both feet flat instead.

The beginner path, in full

Every line in the order above carries a short beginner note. Here is the same path written out as one continuous set of choices, so you can read it before you begin rather than while the timer is running.

  • Take Child's Pose with the knees wide and a folded blanket or a cushion between your heels and your hips if the hips do not reach.
  • Do Cat and Cow seated in a chair with your hands on your knees if kneeling is uncomfortable.
  • In Thread the Needle, keep the hips high over the knees and only rotate as far as the shoulder is happy to go.
  • Take Downward-Facing Dog with both hands flat on a wall at hip height instead, walking the feet back until the torso is horizontal.
  • In Low Lunge, fold a blanket under the back knee and set a book or a block under each hand.
  • In the Standing Forward Fold, bend the knees generously and rest your hands on your shins.
  • Take Tree Pose with the toes of the lifted foot still on the floor and one hand on a wall.
  • Finish in Mountain Pose with your back lightly against a wall.

If you own no props at all

Every position here works on a carpet, a folded towel or a bare floor. Use a folded bath towel under the knees for the kneeling positions and a stack of two hardback books under each hand where a block is suggested. The only thing worth avoiding is a slippery floor in socks, because the standing positions need grip.

How to shorten it

Cut it to six minutes by dropping Thread the Needle on both sides and the two Low Lunges. Cut it to four by keeping only the opening rest, Cat and Cow, the forward fold and Mountain Pose. A four-minute practice done every day beats a ten-minute practice done twice.
