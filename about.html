About Stillpoint

A reference book that happens to be a website.

Who publishes this, how the entries are written, what we refuse to publish, and exactly where the money comes from.

Photograph via Unsplash

What we are trying to do

Most yoga instruction assumes you can already see what the teacher is doing. Video assumes it explicitly. Studio classes assume it implicitly, because the teacher demonstrates and you copy. That works well in a room and badly on your own floor at half past six in the morning, where there is nobody to copy and the shape has to be built from a description.

Stillpoint is written for that second situation. Every entry is meant to be read first and moved to second, so that a posture makes sense in your head before you ask your body to find it. We describe which joints move, which tissues lengthen, where the weight sits, what the breath is doing and what usually goes wrong. We do not describe what a posture is supposed to fix.

The format is deliberately closer to a reference book than to a blog. Entries are laid out identically so that once you know the format you can read any of them quickly. Sequences carry a running time, a props list and a hold time on every line. Nothing is padded to reach a word count and nothing is written to rank for a search query.

Written for real bodies

Arm length, leg length, torso length, hip socket shape and shoulder range vary enormously between people, and that variation is not a defect to be corrected. So every pose entry carries at least two accessible alternatives using a chair, a wall, a block, a strap, a bolster or a blanket, and those alternatives are presented with the same weight as the standard shape. They are not steps toward a real posture. In many cases they are the version worth keeping.

Every sequence carries a full beginner path, written out separately, that uses a chair and a wall throughout and asks nothing of your floor-to-standing transitions.

What we will not publish

  • Medical, therapeutic or health claims of any kind. No posture on this site treats, cures, heals, relieves or prevents anything, and we do not write as though it might.
  • Weight-loss framing. No calorie figures, no fat-burning language, no before-and-after content, no body-shape commentary, and nothing about diets, cleanses, fasting or detoxes.
  • Invented credentials or reviews. Every teacher name, qualification, testimonial and business detail that has not been verified is marked as a placeholder in the page source until it is replaced by something real.
  • Clickbait, fake urgency and fake scarcity. No countdown timers, no invented statistics, no "only three spots left".
  • Anything that presents this site as a clinical authority. We are a reference library. We are not physical therapists, physicians or clinicians, and we do not write as if we were.

How an entry gets written

  1. The shape is described mechanically: which joints move, in which direction, and what carries load.
  2. Entry steps are written from a position the reader is already in, numbered, with nothing assumed.
  3. Cues are written as directions rather than destinations, with a note on when each one does not apply.
  4. At least two prop-supported alternatives are written, using props most people either own or can substitute from a household.
  5. A contraindication note is written listing who should approach with care, with a standing instruction to work with a qualified teacher.
  6. The whole entry is read back against a claims checklist: nothing therapeutic, nothing about weight, nothing that presents Stillpoint as a clinical authority.

If you spot something in an entry that reads as a health claim, or anything factually wrong, tell us. Corrections are made quickly and noted. Email corrections@stillpoint.studio.

How this site is funded

The short version, in plain English. The long version is on the disclosure page.

The pose library, all four sequences, the glossary, the breath guide and every written guide are free to read, with no account and no paywall. Producing and hosting that costs money, and here is exactly where the money comes from.

  1. Display advertising. Third-party ad networks, including Google. Slots are labeled Advertisement, sit below the main content, never touch navigation or buttons, and never appear on legal pages or the error page. Advertising cookies are not set before you accept them.
  2. Optional membership. $6 a month or $54 a year in US dollars, for ad-free browsing, downloadable sequence PDFs, printable pose cards and saved collections. It unlocks no writing. See pricing.
  3. Affiliate commissions. Some links to mats, blocks, straps and bolsters pay a commission if you buy. Each one is disclosed beside the link, not only in the footer.
  4. Paid digital products. Downloadable beginner programs and sequence bundles, bought once, in US dollars.
  5. Sponsored articles. The word Sponsored appears above the headline along with the sponsor's name. Sponsors do not review or approve editorial content elsewhere on the site.
  6. Directory listings. A basic listing in the teacher directory is free. A featured placement is paid and labeled as featured.

What we do not do: sell your email address, sell your personal information, paywall the library, use fake urgency, or run interstitial ads that block content when you arrive.
