Glossary

54 terms, Sanskrit and English.

What the words mean, how they are written with correct diacritics, and where each one appears on this site.

Photograph via Unsplash

On the names

Sanskrit pose names are used in most US studios alongside English ones, and the mix varies enormously from teacher to teacher. Some name every posture in both languages, some use only English, and some use Sanskrit for a handful of shapes and English for the rest. None of these is more correct than the others, and you are not expected to know any of them on a first visit.

The names are written here with diacritical marks, which indicate vowel length and specific consonants. Ā is a long a, as in father. Ś and ṣ are both sh-like sounds. Ṇ, ṭ and ḍ are retroflex, made with the tongue further back than the English equivalents. Ṅ is the ng in sing. You will hear all of these approximated in most American classrooms, and that is fine.

The one practical use of knowing a name is that it lets you look a posture up. If a teacher says a word you did not catch, ask afterwards. Every teacher we have ever met would rather be asked.

The terms

English name first, Sanskrit underneath. Terms that have a full entry elsewhere on this site are linked.

Jump to A B C D E H J K M N P S T U V Y

A

Downward-Facing DogAdho Mukha Śvānāsana
Literally downward-facing dog posture. The inverted V shape with hands and feet on the floor. See the full entry.
Low LungeAñjaneyāsana
A lunge with the back knee resting on the floor. Named for Añjanā, in some tellings the mother of Hanumān.
Happy BabyĀnanda Bālāsana
On the back with the knees drawn toward the armpits and the hands holding the feet.
HalfArdha
A prefix meaning half, as in Ardha Uttānāsana, half forward fold.
Posture, seatĀsana
The physical postures of yoga. It appears as the suffix on almost every pose name. Literally a seat.
High LungeAśva Sañcalanāsana
A lunge with the back knee lifted and the back heel raised.

B

Bound Angle PoseBaddha Koṇāsana
Seated with the soles of the feet together and the knees out to the sides.
Child's PoseBālāsana
Kneeling with the hips back toward the heels and the torso resting forward. See the full entry.
Bind, lockBandha
A muscular engagement used in some traditions. In a general class you will rarely be asked for one explicitly.
Cobra PoseBhujaṅgāsana
Lying face down and lifting the chest by extending the upper back.
Cow PoseBitilāsana
On hands and knees, letting the belly lower and the chest lift. Paired with Cat.

C

Four-Limbed Staff PoseChaturaṅga Daṇḍāsana
Lowering from plank with the elbows bending straight back. Often shortened to chaturanga in a class.
WheelChakra
A term from yoga philosophy. It appears in some pose names and in some teaching traditions.

D

Staff PoseDaṇḍāsana
Sitting with the legs straight out in front and the spine upright. The reference position for seated work.
Gaze pointDṛṣṭi
Where the eyes rest during a posture. Fixing the gaze on a still point makes balance work considerably easier.
Two feetDvi Pāda
A prefix indicating both legs are involved.

E

One footEka Pāda
A prefix indicating one leg, as in Eka Pāda Rājakapotāsana, one-legged pigeon.

H

Force, effortHatha
In modern US studios, a general-pace class in which postures are taught one at a time and held for several breaths. See styles explained.

J

Head-to-Knee Forward BendJānu Śīrṣāsana
Seated with one leg straight and the other foot drawn to the inner thigh, folding over the straight leg.

K

Breath retentionKumbhaka
A pause between the inhale and the exhale. Approach with care and skip it if you are pregnant or have any relevant condition. See breathwork basics.

M

Cat PoseMarjaryāsana
On hands and knees, rounding the spine toward the ceiling. Paired with Cow.
Seal, gestureMudrā
A hand or body position. In a general class this usually refers to what the hands are doing.
A city in IndiaMysore
Also the name for a self-paced Ashtanga class format in which everyone practices their own portion of the series while a teacher moves around the room.

N

A greetingNamaste
Used at the end of many US classes. Its meaning and its use in Western studios are debated; you are not required to say it.
ChannelNāḍī
A term from yoga philosophy describing pathways in the body as traditionally conceived.

P

Seated Forward FoldPaścimottānāsana
Folding forward over straight legs from a seated position.
Side, flankParśva
A prefix indicating a sideways or lateral variation.
Pyramid PosePārśvottanāsana
A short, narrow stance with both legs straight and the torso folding over the front leg.
Plank PosePhalakāsana
A straight line from heels to head with the hands under the shoulders.
Breath practicePrāṇāyāma
The general term for breathing practices. In a US studio it usually means a few minutes of counted breathing. See breathwork basics.
Wide-Legged Forward FoldPrasarita Padottānāsana
A very wide stance with the torso folding forward between the legs.

S

SupportedSalamba
A prefix meaning supported, as in Salamba Bhujaṅgāsana, Sphinx Pose.
Even fluctuationSama vṛtti
An even breath count, the same length in and out.
Locust PoseŚalabhāsana
Face down, lifting the chest, arms and legs away from the floor.
Corpse PoseŚavāsana
Lying flat on the back at the end of a practice. The closing position of almost every class.
Bridge PoseSetu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana
Lying on the back and lifting the hips with the feet planted. See the full entry.
Easy SeatSukhāsana
A simple cross-legged seated position. Sitting on a folded blanket makes it considerably easier.
ReclinedSupta
A prefix meaning lying down, as in Supta Baddha Koṇāsana.
Supine TwistSupta Matsyendrāsana
On the back, drawing the knees across the body while the opposite shoulder stays heavy.

T

Mountain PoseTāḍāsana
Standing upright with the weight even across both feet. The reference position for standing work. See the full entry.
Triangle PoseTrikoṇāsana
A wide straight-legged stance with the torso tipping sideways over the front leg.

U

Victorious breathUjjāyī
Breathing through the nose with a slight narrowing at the back of the throat, producing a soft audible sound. Used to set a shared tempo.
UpwardŪrdhva
A prefix meaning upward, as in Ūrdhva Mukha Śvānāsana, Upward-Facing Dog.
Camel PoseUṣṭrāsana
Kneeling upright and extending the upper back with the hands reaching toward the heels.
Chair PoseUtkaṭāsana
Standing with the knees bent and the hips lowered as if to sit.
Standing Forward FoldUttānāsana
Folding forward from standing with the spine long.
ExtendedUtthita
A prefix meaning extended or stretched out.

V

Uneven fluctuationViṣama vṛtti
An uneven breath count, most commonly a longer exhale than inhale.
To place in a special wayVinyāsa
In modern usage, the linking of postures to the breath so that you move continuously. Also the name of a class style.
Legs Up the WallViparīta Karaṇī
Lying on the back with the legs resting vertically up a wall.
Warrior PoseVīrabhadrāsana
Named for Vīrabhadra, a figure from Hindu mythology. Numbered I, II and III. See the Warrior II entry.
Tree PoseVṛkṣāsana
Standing on one leg with the other foot against the standing leg. See the full entry.

Y

A style nameYin
A class format built on long floor holds of two to five minutes with generous prop use. See styles explained.
To yoke, to joinYoga
The umbrella term. In a US studio it almost always refers to the physical postures and the breathing practices that accompany them.
