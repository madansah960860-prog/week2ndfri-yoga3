Practice

No, and flexibility is not a prerequisite for anything on this site. Range of motion is one of the things a regular practice slowly changes, and it varies between people partly because of skeletal proportion, which does not change at all. Every forward fold on this site is shown with a bent-knee version and a block version, and both are the real posture.

Ten minutes. The 10-Minute Morning is written specifically as a first practice: twelve positions, a mat and nothing else, and it can be done in whatever you slept in. Ten minutes done four or five times a week accumulates into far more than sixty minutes planned for Sunday and then rescheduled.

Use the chair path. The entire Desk Reset is built around a chair and a wall, and every other sequence on this site carries a chair variation for every position in its order. Chair versions are shown with the same weight as floor versions throughout this site because they are the same postures worked from a different height.

Stop. There is a difference between the broad, spread-out feeling of a muscle lengthening, which fades within seconds of coming out of a shape, and a sharp, pinching, burning or joint-located sensation, which does not. The second is a signal to come out immediately and not to push through. If a sensation persists after you leave the shape, appears in a joint, travels down a limb, or is still there the next day, stop practicing and speak with a qualified healthcare professional. Read the health and safety disclaimer.

Use the three-question check: can you breathe evenly in the shape, is the sensation broad rather than sharp, and can you come out of it under control. If all three are true you are close enough for a home practice. For anything more precise you need a qualified teacher in the room, because alignment feedback requires someone to see you move.

No. Most classes suggest it because nasal breathing naturally slows the breath and makes it easy to keep time with, but it is a convention rather than a rule. If your nose is blocked or nose breathing makes you uncomfortable, breathe through your mouth. See breathwork basics.

Practice safely

Stillpoint publishes general movement education, not medical advice. Nothing here treats, prevents or improves any medical condition. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition, and stop if you feel pain. Work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance, because a website cannot see how you move.
