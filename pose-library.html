The pose library

Thirty-eight postures, indexed and explained.

Filter by difficulty, by the part of the body a posture asks the most of, and by the prop you actually own. Every entry carries a first-day option.

Photograph via Unsplash

How to use this index

This is a reference index, not a curriculum. Nothing here needs to be worked through in order. Find the posture you saw in a class, read what it asks of you, and take the first-day option listed underneath it if the standard version is not available to you today.

Six entries carry a full breakdown plate with numbered entry steps, alignment cues, a breath pattern, a suggested hold, common mistakes, prop-supported alternatives, a progression and a contraindication note. Those six are linked. Every other entry gives you the shape, what it asks of you, and how to make it workable straight away.

Difficulty here describes the joint range, balance and load tolerance the standard version assumes. It is not a ranking of value. A supported posture held well teaches you more than an unsupported posture you are fighting.

Reading the filters

Difficulty refers to the standard version only. Every posture has an accessible version at every level.

Body area is where the posture asks for the most range or the most work, not the only place you will feel it.

Prop lists the props that appear in that entry's options. Choosing No props needed shows postures whose standard version needs nothing but a floor.

The index

Thirty-eight postures with English and Sanskrit names, difficulty, body area, prop options and a first-day alternative for each.

Showing all 38 poses in the library.

  • Corpse Pose

    Śavāsana

    Lying flat on the back with the arms a little away from the sides and the legs relaxed outward. It is the closing position of almost every practice and the one people are most likely to skip. Nothing is held, nothing is lengthened, and the only instruction is to stay still for a few minutes.

    First-day option. Roll a blanket under the knees so the low back settles, and put a second folded blanket over you.

    BeginnerSpineNo props needed

  • Mountain Pose

    Tāḍāsana

    Standing upright with the feet roughly hip-width apart, weight spread across both feet, arms alongside the body. It looks like doing nothing and it is the reference position that every other standing shape is measured against.

    First-day option. Stand with your back lightly touching a wall so you can feel where your weight actually sits.

    BeginnerLegs, BalanceNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Standing Forward Fold

    Uttānāsana

    From standing, folding forward at the hip joints with the spine long, letting the head hang. The back of the legs lengthens and the spine is unloaded. Where your hands land is a matter of proportion, not achievement.

    First-day option. Bend the knees generously and rest your hands on two blocks or on a chair seat.

    BeginnerHamstrings, SpineNo props needed

  • Half Forward Fold

    Ardha Uttānāsana

    Halfway up from a forward fold, hands on the shins or on blocks, the spine drawn into a long flat line from the tailbone to the crown. It is a spacer position that teaches you what a long spine feels like before you fold again.

    First-day option. Hands on a chair seat, feet a stride back, so the torso can reach horizontal without hamstring range.

    BeginnerHamstrings, SpineNo props needed

  • Downward-Facing Dog

    Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

    Hands and feet on the floor, hips lifted, body forming an inverted V. The shoulders carry load overhead and the back line of the legs lengthens. Bent knees are the normal starting version for most people, not a compromise.

    First-day option. Put your hands on a chair seat or flat on a wall at hip height and walk the feet back instead.

    BeginnerShoulders, Hamstrings, CoreNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Plank Pose

    Phalakāsana

    A straight line from heels to head with the hands under the shoulders and the whole front of the body working to keep the middle from sagging. It is a holding position, not a movement.

    First-day option. Take it from the knees, or set your hands on a chair seat and angle the body up.

    IntermediateCore, ShouldersNo props needed

  • Four-Limbed Staff Pose

    Chaturaṅga Daṇḍāsana

    Lowering from plank with the elbows bending straight back and staying over the wrists, stopping when the upper arms are roughly parallel to the floor. It asks for considerable shoulder and midsection control and is the position most often rushed.

    First-day option. Lower the knees first, then bend the elbows, and stop halfway rather than going to the floor.

    AdvancedShoulders, CoreNo props needed

  • Cobra Pose

    Bhujaṅgāsana

    Lying face down, hands under the shoulders, lifting the chest by extending the upper back while the legs stay heavy. The height of the lift is far less important than where the bend happens along the spine.

    First-day option. Lift only to the point where your hands still feel light on the floor, and keep the elbows bent.

    BeginnerSpine, ShouldersNo props needed

  • Upward-Facing Dog

    Ūrdhva Mukha Śvānāsana

    Arms straight, chest lifted, thighs and knees off the floor, weight in the hands and the tops of the feet. It asks more of the shoulders and the front of the hips than Cobra does.

    First-day option. Practice Cobra instead until the shoulders can hold the chest up without the neck taking over.

    IntermediateSpine, Shoulders, CoreNo props needed

  • Locust Pose

    Śalabhāsana

    Face down, lifting the chest, arms and legs away from the floor at the same time, with nothing to push against. It is the position that most clearly shows how much of a backbend comes from the back of the body rather than the arms.

    First-day option. Lift only the chest and arms, and leave the legs entirely on the floor.

    IntermediateSpine, CoreNo props needed

  • Child's Pose

    Bālāsana

    Kneeling with the hips settling back toward the heels and the torso folding forward, arms extended or alongside the body. It is a resting position that can be held for several minutes at a time.

    First-day option. Put a folded blanket behind the knees and a bolster lengthwise under the chest.

    BeginnerHips, SpineNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Cat Pose

    Marjaryāsana

    On hands and knees, rounding the spine toward the ceiling and letting the head release, usually on an exhale. Half of a paired movement with Cow Pose.

    First-day option. Sit in a chair with your hands on your knees and round the spine from there.

    BeginnerSpine, CoreNo props needed

  • Cow Pose

    Bitilāsana

    On hands and knees, letting the belly lower and the chest lift, usually on an inhale. Paired with Cat Pose to move the spine into flexion and extension in time with the breath.

    First-day option. Do it seated in a chair, or place a folded blanket under the knees if kneeling is uncomfortable.

    BeginnerSpineNo props needed

  • Thread the Needle

    Parśva Bālāsana

    From hands and knees, sliding one arm under the body along the floor and letting that shoulder and the side of the head rest down. The upper back rotates while the hips stay square.

    First-day option. Keep the hips high over the knees and only rotate as far as the shoulder is happy to go.

    BeginnerShoulders, SpineNo props needed

  • Warrior II

    Vīrabhadrāsana II

    A wide stance with the front knee bent over the ankle, the back foot turned in, and the arms extended level with the shoulders. The front hip folds while the back leg stays long.

    First-day option. Shorten the stance by about a foot and bend the front knee only halfway.

    BeginnerLegs, Hips, BalanceNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Warrior I

    Vīrabhadrāsana I

    A lunge-shaped stance with the back foot turned in, the hips angled forward and the arms overhead. It asks the back hip to rotate forward and the upper back to extend at the same time.

    First-day option. Widen your stance sideways so both feet sit on their own rail, and keep the hands on the hips.

    BeginnerLegs, Hips, ShouldersNo props needed

  • Extended Side Angle

    Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana

    From a wide stance with the front knee bent, the torso leans out over the front thigh with the forearm on the thigh or a hand toward the floor and the top arm reaching overhead.

    First-day option. Rest the forearm on the front thigh, or set a block under the lower hand at its tallest height.

    IntermediateLegs, Hips, CoreNo props needed

  • Triangle Pose

    Utthita Trikoṇāsana

    A wide straight-legged stance with the torso tipping sideways over the front leg, one hand reaching down and the other up. The lengthening is felt along the underside of the torso and the inside of the front leg.

    First-day option. Put a block on its tallest setting under the lower hand rather than reaching for the shin or floor.

    BeginnerLegs, Hips, SpineNo props needed

  • Chair Pose

    Utkaṭāsana

    Standing with the knees bent and the hips lowered as if to sit, torso leaning slightly forward, arms reaching up or forward. The front of the thighs works hard almost immediately.

    First-day option. Stand with your back against a wall and slide down only a few inches.

    BeginnerLegs, CoreNo props needed

  • Tree Pose

    Vṛkṣāsana

    Standing on one leg with the other foot resting against the calf or inner thigh of the standing leg, never against the side of the knee. The standing foot makes constant small corrections.

    First-day option. Keep the toes of the lifted foot on the floor and rest one hand on a wall.

    BeginnerBalance, Hips, LegsNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Low Lunge

    Añjaneyāsana

    One foot forward with the knee bent, the back knee down on the floor, hips settling forward. The front of the back hip lengthens. Often the first place people feel the effect of a day spent sitting.

    First-day option. Fold a blanket under the back knee and put a block under each hand for height.

    BeginnerHips, LegsNo props needed

  • High Lunge

    Aśva Sañcalāsana

    A lunge with the back knee lifted and the back heel raised, torso upright, arms overhead or at the hips. It demands more from the back leg and from balance than the low version.

    First-day option. Set your fingertips on two blocks either side of the front foot, or drop the back knee down.

    IntermediateLegs, Hips, Balance, CoreNo props needed

  • Pyramid Pose

    Pārśvottanāsana

    A short, narrow stance with both legs straight and the torso folding over the front leg. It is a hamstring-dominant shape that punishes a rounded low back, which is why blocks are close to mandatory here.

    First-day option. Hands on two blocks or on a chair seat, front knee softly bent, torso stopping at horizontal.

    IntermediateHamstrings, HipsProps required

  • Wide-Legged Forward Fold

    Prasarita Padottānāsana

    A very wide stance with the torso folding forward between the legs and the hands on the floor or on blocks. Wider feet make the fold easier, not harder.

    First-day option. Set two blocks under the hands at their tallest setting and keep a soft bend in both knees.

    BeginnerHamstrings, Hips, SpineNo props needed

  • Standing Half Forward Bend at the Wall

    Ardha Uttānāsana (wall)

    Hands flat on a wall at roughly hip height, feet walked back until the torso is horizontal and the arms are straight. It gives most of the shoulder work of Downward-Facing Dog with none of the weight in the hands.

    First-day option. This is already the accessible version. Bend the knees as much as you like and step closer to the wall.

    BeginnerShoulders, Hamstrings, SpineProps required

  • Bridge Pose

    Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

    Lying on the back with the knees bent and the feet planted, lifting the hips away from the floor. The back of the legs works and the front of the hips lengthens.

    First-day option. Lift the hips and slide a block underneath the sacrum, then rest your full weight on it.

    BeginnerLegs, Spine, CoreNo props needed

    Read the full breakdown

  • Reclined Bound Angle

    Supta Baddha Koṇāsana

    Lying on the back with the soles of the feet together and the knees falling open, usually with support under each thigh. A supported resting shape that can be held for five minutes or more.

    First-day option. Put a block or a rolled blanket under each thigh so the legs are held rather than hanging.

    BeginnerHipsProps required

  • Supine Twist

    Supta Matsyendrāsana

    Lying on the back, drawing one or both knees across the body while the opposite shoulder stays heavy. The spine rotates in a position where it carries almost no load.

    First-day option. Place a folded blanket or a bolster under the knees so they land on something instead of hanging.

    BeginnerSpine, HipsNo props needed

  • Happy Baby

    Ānanda Bālāsana

    On the back, knees drawn toward the armpits, hands holding the outer edges of the feet or the backs of the thighs. The inner thighs and the back of the hips lengthen.

    First-day option. Loop a strap around each foot and hold the strap instead of reaching for the feet.

    BeginnerHipsNo props needed

  • Legs Up the Wall

    Viparīta Karaṇī

    Lying on the back with the hips near a wall and the legs resting vertically up it. There is no muscular effort involved; the wall does the work of holding the legs.

    First-day option. Slide a folded blanket under the hips and move a few inches further from the wall.

    BeginnerLegs, HipsProps required

  • Staff Pose

    Daṇḍāsana

    Sitting on the floor with the legs straight out in front and the spine upright. It is the seated equivalent of Mountain Pose and the reference position for the seated shapes.

    First-day option. Sit on the edge of one or two folded blankets so the hips are higher than the knees.

    BeginnerCore, Spine, HamstringsProps required

  • Seated Forward Fold

    Paścimottānāsana

    From Staff Pose, folding forward over straight legs from the hip joints. It is far more demanding than it looks and is the shape most often forced.

    First-day option. Sit up on a folded blanket, bend the knees, and loop a strap around the feet to hold instead of reaching.

    IntermediateHamstrings, SpineProps required

  • Head-to-Knee Forward Bend

    Jānu Śīrṣāsana

    Seated with one leg straight and the other foot drawn to the inner thigh, folding over the straight leg. Working one leg at a time makes the differences between your two sides obvious.

    First-day option. Blanket under the hips, strap around the extended foot, and a block or cushion under the bent knee.

    BeginnerHamstrings, Hips, SpineProps required

  • Bound Angle Pose

    Baddha Koṇāsana

    Seated with the soles of the feet together and the knees out to the sides. Where the knees land is largely a matter of hip socket shape and is not something to force downward.

    First-day option. Sit up on a folded blanket and place a block under each knee so the legs are supported.

    BeginnerHipsProps required

  • Half Lord of the Fishes

    Ardha Matsyendrāsana

    A seated twist with one foot crossed over the opposite thigh and the torso rotating toward the top knee. The rotation should come from the middle of the back, not from pulling on the arm.

    First-day option. Keep the bottom leg straight, sit on a blanket, and hold the top knee rather than levering against it.

    IntermediateSpine, HipsProps required

  • Pigeon Pose Preparation

    Eka Pāda Rājakapotāsana (prep)

    The front shin angled across the mat with the back leg extended behind, torso upright or folding forward. It is a strong outer-hip shape and one of the most frequently overdone positions in a home practice.

    First-day option. Take it lying on your back with the ankle crossed over the opposite thigh instead, or use a chair seat.

    IntermediateHipsProps required

  • Camel Pose

    Uṣṭrāsana

    Kneeling upright and extending the upper back while the hands reach toward the heels or rest on the low back. It asks for a lot of upper back extension and is easy to take too far.

    First-day option. Keep the hands on the low back, tuck the toes so the heels are higher, and set blocks beside each heel.

    IntermediateSpine, ShouldersProps required

  • Sphinx Pose

    Salamba Bhujaṅgāsana

    Lying face down on the forearms with the elbows under or slightly ahead of the shoulders. A low, sustainable back extension that can be held far longer than Cobra.

    First-day option. Slide a bolster under the chest and rest on it, letting the arms be almost entirely passive.

    BeginnerSpine, ShouldersNo props needed
