The sequence index

Four practices, chosen by the time you have.

Eight minutes at a desk, ten in the morning, fifteen in the evening, thirty on a good day. Every one carries hold times, props and a beginner path.

Photograph via Unsplash

How to choose one

Pick by the time you genuinely have, not the time you would like to have. This is the single most reliable way to end up with a practice that lasts more than three weeks. Ten minutes done five mornings a week accumulates. Sixty minutes planned for Sunday and then rescheduled does not.

Every sequence on this site is laid out the same way: a total running time, a difficulty reading, the props required, a position-by-position order with a hold time on every line, and a beginner path written out separately in full. Each one also carries a timer that counts the holds and moves on by itself, and a print stylesheet, so you can put a page on the floor next to you instead of watching a screen.

The four here are deliberately different in kind rather than in difficulty. The Desk Reset needs no mat and no floor. The Evening Wind-Down is entirely supported and involves no standing at all. The Morning is short and brisk. The Gentle Full Practice is the long one for the day you have room for it.

If you are completely new, read Start Here first, then use the 10-Minute Morning for a fortnight before adding anything else.

Reading a sequence page

Total duration is the sum of every hold. It does not include setting up props, so allow two or three minutes on top for the supported sequences.

Difficulty describes the standard version. Every position has a beginner path listed on the same line.

Props required lists what the standard version uses. Household substitutes are given on each page and in the props guide.

The four sequences

Each entry below carries the full order and the beginner path. The linked page adds the working timer and the print layout.

  • 10-Minute Morning

    10 minutesBeginner12 positionsA mat. Nothing else.

    Twelve positions that move the spine through flexion, extension, rotation and side-lengthening in the first four minutes, then put weight through the hands and feet, then finish standing. It ends standing on purpose, because a practice that ends lying down at seven in the morning is one you have to negotiate your way out of.

    The order.

    • Child's Pose, 60 sec
    • Cat and Cow, 60 sec
    • Thread the Needle both sides, 45 sec each
    • Downward-Facing Dog, 60 sec
    • Low Lunge both sides, 60 sec each
    • Standing Forward Fold, 45 sec
    • Mountain Pose, 45 sec
    • Tree Pose both sides, 40 sec each
    • Mountain Pose to finish, 40 sec

    Beginner path. Take Downward-Facing Dog with the palms flat on a wall, keep the toes of the lifted foot on the floor in Tree Pose, and put a blanket under the back knee in the lunges.

    Open the sequence and timer

  • Desk Reset

    8 minutesAll levels11 positionsA sturdy chair and a clear wall.

    Eleven seated and standing positions built entirely around a chair and a wall, for shoulders and hips that have been folded into the same angle since morning. No mat, no floor, no change of clothes. The first six positions are seated and take four minutes on their own.

    The order.

    • Seated Mountain Pose, 45 sec
    • Seated Cat and Cow, 60 sec
    • Seated side bends, 30 sec each
    • Seated twists, 45 sec each
    • Wall chest opener, 45 sec
    • Standing half forward bend at the wall, 60 sec
    • Standing figure-four at the chair, 45 sec each
    • Seated close, 30 sec

    Beginner path. Do the first six positions seated and finish with the closing breath. That is a complete four-minute practice on its own.

    Open the sequence and timer

  • Evening Wind-Down

    15 minutesBeginner8 positionsBolster, blanket, block and a wall.

    Eight supported positions held for ninety seconds to three minutes each. The length is the design: short holds ask you to arrive in a shape, long holds ask you to stay in it after the novelty wears off. Everything is done on the floor and it works in a dim room.

    The order.

    • Supported Child's Pose, 90 sec each side
    • Reclined Bound Angle, 3 min
    • Supine twists, 90 sec each
    • Supported Bridge on a block, 2 min 30 sec
    • Legs Up the Wall, 2 min
    • Corpse Pose, 90 sec

    Beginner path. Raise the supports higher so the legs are held rather than hanging, and lie on your side with a bolster between the knees if lying flat is uncomfortable.

    Open the sequence and timer

  • Gentle Full Practice

    30 minutesBeginner to intermediate22 positionsMat, two blocks, a strap and a wall.

    Twenty-two positions covering standing, balance, seated, prone and supine work, with two scheduled rests and a full three-minute close. The pace is unhurried, which is not the same as easy: standing holds run to ninety seconds, and the legs will have opinions about that.

    The order.

    • Child's Pose, 2 min
    • Cat and Cow, 90 sec
    • Thread the Needle both sides, 60 sec each
    • Downward-Facing Dog, 60 sec
    • Standing Forward Fold and Mountain Pose, 60 sec each
    • Warrior II both sides, 90 sec each
    • Triangle both sides, 75 sec each
    • Tree Pose both sides, 60 sec each
    • Child's Pose rest, 90 sec
    • Low Lunge both sides, 90 sec each
    • Bridge Pose, 2 min
    • Supine twists, 75 sec each
    • Bound Angle and Seated Forward Fold, 60 sec each
    • Corpse Pose, 3 min

    Beginner path. Take every beginner option and you have a complete thirty-minute practice that uses a wall and a chair throughout and asks nothing of your floor-to-standing transitions.

    Open the sequence and timer
