Sequence · 15 min

Fifteen minutes, held long and low.

Eight supported positions held long enough that you stop fidgeting. Bolster, blanket, block and a wall. It ends lying down, which is where it should end.

Go to the timer

Photograph via Unsplash

About this practice

The positions in this sequence are held for ninety seconds to three minutes each, which is considerably longer than in the other sequences on this site. That length is the whole design. Short holds ask you to arrive in a shape; long holds ask you to stay in it after the novelty has worn off, which is a different and quieter kind of attention.

Long holds only work if the props are genuinely doing the holding. If a shape requires any muscular effort to maintain, three minutes will be unpleasant rather than restful. Spend a minute setting the props up before you start the timer. Move the bolster. Add a second blanket. Get it right and then stop adjusting.

Long holds only work if the props are doing the holding. Set them up before the timer starts. Evening Wind-Down

Everything here is done on the floor, and the sequence works in a dim room. There is no standing, no balancing and nothing that requires you to be warm first.

We make no claims about what this sequence does beyond the physical: joints move through range, muscles that have been working all day are put in positions where they do not have to work, and you spend fifteen minutes doing one thing. That is the honest description of it.

The essentials

Total duration. 15 min

Difficulty. Beginner

Positions. 8

Props required. A bolster or firm cushion, one blanket, one block and a clear wall.

Space needed. About 7 feet by 4 feet, with wall access.

Best time to use it. Late evening, on the floor, in the dark if you like.

The order, position by position

Total running time 15 min. Every line carries its hold time and a beginner path.

  1. 1 min 30 sec Supported Child's Pose, head to the right Bālāsana Bolster lengthwise between the thighs, torso resting on it, head turned to the right. Knees wide, blanket behind the knees. Beginner path: Add a second cushion under the chest if the bolster is too low.
  2. 1 min 30 sec Supported Child's Pose, head to the left Bālāsana Turn the head to the other side without lifting the torso. Let the arms stay wherever they are comfortable. Beginner path: If kneeling is uncomfortable, lie on your side with the bolster between your knees instead.
  3. 3 min Reclined Bound Angle Supta Baddha Koṇāsana On your back, soles of the feet together, knees falling open with a block or rolled blanket under each thigh. Beginner path: Raise the supports higher so the legs are held closer to the body.
  4. 1 min 30 sec Supine twist, knees to the right Supta Matsyendrāsana Knees drawn in, then lowered to the right onto a blanket or bolster. Left shoulder stays heavy on the floor. Beginner path: Put the bolster under the knees so they rest on it instead of hanging.
  5. 1 min 30 sec Supine twist, knees to the left Supta Matsyendrāsana Second side. Turn the head the opposite way only if the neck is comfortable doing so. Beginner path: Put the bolster under the knees and keep the head facing straight up.
  6. 2 min 30 sec Supported Bridge on a block Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Lift the hips, slide the block under the bony back of the pelvis at its lowest height, and let your whole weight rest on it. Beginner path: Use a folded blanket instead of a block for a lower, softer support.
  7. 2 min Legs Up the Wall Viparīta Karaṇī Hips near the wall, legs resting vertically up it, arms out to the sides. No muscular effort at all. Beginner path: Move a few inches further from the wall and put a folded blanket under the hips.
  8. 1 min 30 sec Corpse Pose Śavāsana Flat on the back, arms a little away from the sides, legs relaxed outward. Stay still and let the timer end it. Beginner path: Blanket roll under the knees and a second blanket over you.

The beginner path, in full

Every line in the order above carries a short beginner note. Here is the same path written out as one continuous set of choices, so you can read it before you begin rather than while the timer is running.

  • Set the bolster up for supported Child's Pose before you begin: lengthwise between the thighs, running from the pelvis toward the head, with a blanket behind the knees.
  • For Reclined Bound Angle, put a block or a rolled blanket under each thigh so the legs are held rather than hanging open.
  • In the supine twists, place a folded blanket or the bolster under the knees so they land on something rather than pulling on the low back.
  • For the supported Bridge, use the block on its lowest height under the bony back of the pelvis, not under the low back.
  • For Legs Up the Wall, sit sideways to the wall, then swing the legs up as you lie back. Move a few inches further from the wall if the back of the legs objects.
  • In the final rest, roll a blanket under the knees and put a second blanket over you. Bodies cool down quickly when they stop moving.
  • If lying flat on the back is not comfortable, do the whole sequence on your side with the bolster between your knees, or ask a qualified teacher for a version suited to you.

If you own no props at all

A firm sofa cushion under a folded blanket is a serviceable bolster. Two hardback books are a block. Any blanket you own is a blanket. The wall is the only item with no substitute, and most rooms have one that is clear enough once a chair is moved.

How to shorten it

Take the first two positions and the final rest for a seven-minute version. If you have five minutes, do Legs Up the Wall for four of them and the final rest for one.
