Speak with a healthcare professional first

Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice, including anything described on this site. This is particularly important if you:

  • are pregnant, may be pregnant, or have recently given birth;
  • have been injured recently, or have an injury that has not fully resolved;
  • are recovering from surgery of any kind;
  • are managing any ongoing health condition, including but not limited to conditions affecting the heart, blood pressure, joints, bones, spine, eyes, inner ear, nervous system or breathing;
  • experience dizziness, fainting or balance difficulties;
  • take any medication that affects balance, blood pressure, heart rate or alertness;
  • have not been physically active for a long period;
  • are over the age of 65 and new to physical activity.

A healthcare professional can assess you. We cannot. We have never seen you, we do not know your history, and no amount of written detail changes that. Where the advice you are given conflicts with anything on this site, follow the advice you were given by the person who examined you.

This site is written for adults. It is not designed for children, and children should practice only under the supervision of a qualified teacher and with a parent or guardian's agreement.

Stop if you feel pain

If something hurts, stop. That instruction is not conditional and it is not something to breathe through, work around or push past.

There is a difference between the broad, dull, spread-out sensation of a muscle being lengthened, which fades within seconds of leaving a shape, and a sharp, pinching, burning, electric or joint-located sensation, which does not. The first is ordinary. The second is a signal to come out of the position immediately.

Stop practicing and seek medical attention if you experience any of the following:

  • pain that is sharp, sudden, or located in a joint rather than in muscle;
  • pain, numbness, tingling or weakness that travels down an arm or a leg;
  • discomfort that is still present the day after practicing;
  • chest pain, pressure or tightness, or an irregular heartbeat;
  • severe shortness of breath, or breathlessness out of proportion to the effort;
  • dizziness, light-headedness, visual disturbance or fainting;
  • a severe or unusual headache.

If you think you are having a medical emergency, call 911 or your local emergency number immediately. Do not delay seeking medical help because of anything you have read on this site.

Contraindication notes and how to read them

Every pose entry on this site carries a section headed Approach with care, or skip. It lists the circumstances in which that particular shape is commonly treated with caution.

Those notes are general and they are not exhaustive. They are not an assessment of you, they cannot account for your particular history, and the absence of your situation from a list is not a statement that a posture is safe for you. Only a qualified healthcare professional who can examine you is in a position to say that.

Read a contraindication note as a prompt to ask a question, not as permission to proceed. If a note mentions something that applies to you, treat it as a reason to ask a professional before you practice the shape.

Work with a qualified teacher

The single thing a website structurally cannot do is watch you move. It cannot see that your front knee is drifting inward, that one shoulder is riding higher than the other, or that what you are calling a hamstring stretch is happening in your lower back. That feedback requires a qualified teacher in the room with you, and we recommend working with one for hands-on guidance, particularly when you are starting out.

The teacher directory is a starting point for finding one. We do not verify credentials, qualifications, insurance or registration status for anyone listed there, and you should check those yourself before booking.

What we are not

We want to be entirely unambiguous about this.

  • We are not physicians. We do not diagnose, treat or prescribe.
  • We are not physical therapists, physiotherapists, chiropractors, osteopaths or clinicians of any kind. Nothing on this site is rehabilitation, therapy or a treatment plan.
  • We are not your teacher. We cannot assess you, adapt anything to you individually, or give you personal instruction, and we will not attempt to by email.
  • We do not offer individual advice. Please do not send us descriptions of symptoms, injuries or conditions. We are not able to help and we would rather say so plainly than reply with something unhelpful.

Any teaching credential shown anywhere on this site is currently a placeholder for a real, verifiable qualification and is marked as such in the page. No credential shown should be relied on as evidence of anyone's qualifications until it has been verified and the placeholder marking removed.

No weight or body claims

This site publishes nothing about weight loss, calorie expenditure, fat burning, body transformation or body shape, and it does not pair the practice with dietary restriction, cleanses, fasting or detox content of any kind. That is a permanent editorial position rather than a current absence.

Assumption of risk

Yoga is a physical activity, and physical activity carries a risk of injury. By using the material on this site you acknowledge and accept that:

  • you are choosing voluntarily to take part in physical activity and you assume the risks of doing so, including the risk of injury;
  • you are responsible for deciding what is appropriate for your own body on any given day;
  • you are responsible for practicing in a safe space, on a surface that does not slip, with enough clearance around you;
  • you will stop when something hurts and will seek medical attention when it is warranted;
  • you will not rely on this site in place of professional medical advice.
